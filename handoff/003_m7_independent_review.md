# Independent review of M7 (design doc, handoff 003, decision log)

Reviewer: Claude Opus 5.5, read-only. Test suite rerun: **77 passed** (15 map, 5 rng, 4 ruleset, 36 wave, 17 kernel), matching handoff 003 §3.4. Extra read-only computations were run in memory (no files written): `emerged()` of `experiments/m7/waves/scenarios.py` at dt 0.5 and 0.25, the cohort tower-farm and cost cases, the kernel reinforcement sweep, and the gank reaction ticks.

Severity counts: 3 defects, 11 should-fix, 7 judgment calls, 12 notes.

## Findings

### Defects

1. **Defect. The freeze negative is contradicted at the M8 starting tick by the prototype's own criterion.** Files: `docs/design/M7_architecture.md` §1 (line 33), §5.6 "What they do not produce" (lines 248-257), §11.2 R1 (line 808); handoff F12 (line 97, "Verified as a negative result"); decision log TSV line 26. What is wrong: every case verdict and the `emerged` block in `waves.json` is computed at `REPORT_DT = 1.0` (`scenarios.py` line 45, top-level `"dt": 1.0`). The document chose 0.5 s for M8 and calls 1.0 s too coarse (§5.2), but never says that its wave tables are 1.0 s results. Evidence: rerunning `emerged(measure(RECOMMENDED, CASES, dt))` with the shipped code gives `freeze: true` at dt 0.5 (`k=6 hold`, minimum over 5 seeds 194 s of 240 s, with both controls at most 60 s) and `freeze: true` at dt 0.25 (`k=3 hold` minimum 139 s, `k=6 hold` 217.5 s, `k=6 thin over 3` and `thin over 4`). The honest statement is that, at the tick M8 will use, a 6-caster hold meets the pre-registered freeze test, 3 and 4 casters do not, and the holds that work cost the champion about 12,800 to 21,200 damage in 240 s (see item 6). That is a different conflict from "freezes did not emerge", and the owner should see it in that form.

2. **Defect. The gank evidence for the 2 s decision interval is misdescribed, and it points the other way.** Files: doc §5.2 (line 157): "a laner with 390 health under the enemy tower died when both sides decided every second and survived on 30 health when both decided every 5 s (Tested, `q2_fights.d_gank`)". Decision log TSV line 24 cites it as one of three reasons for the starting clock. Evidence: `experiments/m7/kernel/scenarios.py` `gank()` (lines 172-191) builds the world with `staged()`, which fixes `decision_interval=1` (line 28). Only the laner's retreat order is gated by `w.tick % decision_interval == 0`. The jungler's FIGHT order is submitted once before the loop, and red top is ordered every tick. `jungler_first_seen_s` is 14.0 in every case, so "interval 5" means the laner reacts at tick 15 instead of tick 14: a 1 s delay, not a 5 s cadence. The later reaction survived. Interval 2 would also react at tick 14. So the run does not test decision intervals, does not involve "both sides", and does not show that a long interval lets ganks succeed. The argument for 2 s now rests on the sight-range arithmetic alone.

3. **Defect. Silent departure from the owner's timing decision: intents no longer end when they complete.** Owner: "Intents persist until completed or changed." Doc §7.2 (lines 484-491): "When an intent reaches its end condition, the kernel records an outcome... The order still stands." §7.3 (line 498): a RECALL hit by damage "starts again from zero" by itself. A CAMP order attacks the camp again when it respawns, and a FIGHT order stays after `KILLED`. Candidate A (`design_candidate_A.md`, Intent section) matched the owner: "persists until DONE or replaced". Decision log TSV line 21 shows why it changed: a switch to HOLD on completion made the fixed-intent interval test fail, so the semantics were changed until the test passed. Neither the document nor handoff §4 (Departures, lines 134-149) presents this as a departure from an owner decision. R12 (line 819) calls the recall restart "Assumed semantics" but does not link it to the decision. The owner's prompt asks for exactly this case to be explained rather than changed silently. The change also makes the interval-invariance test close to tautological (item 23).

### Should-fix

4. **Should-fix. The slow-push scenario cannot produce a slow push.** Files: `experiments/m7/waves/scenarios.py` `slow_push()` (lines 334-353) and `emerged()` (line 557, `thin["wave"]["median"] >= 12`). Evidence: the built state puts the waves 1,050 units in front of the blue top outer tower (front at s = 9,834). That leaves 2,996 units, an 8.6 s walk, to the red tower's range. The run stops on the first tick that any blue minion enters that range. The next red wave, spawned at 180 s, needs about 26 s to reach that zone, and the next blue wave about 35 s. At dt 0.5 the thinned wave arrives in 24.0 s with 3 minions in all 5 seeds, before any second blue wave can exist near it. A ≥12-minion arrival is therefore close to impossible by construction. Only one wave is thinned, and the red laner is absent or last-hits. The slow-push half of F12 is better supported by `S1b one caster short`: a one-caster advantage advances over about 3.5 wave cycles and reaches the red tower at 104 s (mid) or 114 s (top) with 5 minions. The document files that case under instability. Re-cite S1b for "no stacked wave", or rebuild S4b to thin over several waves starting near the centre.

5. **Should-fix. Wave tables mix tick lengths and overstate tick robustness.** Doc §5.6 tables (lines 239-255), R2 (line 809). (a) "219 to 225 of 240 s at ticks of 0.5 s and below. With the champion absent it stays 101 s, and with a push 23 s": the 101 and 23 are the dt 1.0 controls. At dt 0.5 they are 59.5 and 14.5, and at dt 0.25 they are 36.25 and 14.5. (b) "Lateral slots... 6 of 10 runs": this is dt 1.0. At dt 0.5 it is 5 of 10, and at the 0.25 reference it is 2 of 10. (c) R2 says "Timing metrics stay within 10 percent", but `S7b_tick_sensitivity` shows `S4b last hit, red absent / arrive_s` at +33 percent for dt 0.5 (70.5 s against 53.0 s). (d) Hold times are not converged at 0.5 s (k=3: 106 s, against 222 s at 0.25), and no run finer than 0.25 s exists to show that 0.25 is converged. State the tick for every row.

6. **Should-fix. The freeze "cost" comes from the standing rule, not from emergent mechanics.** `scenarios.py` ASSUMPTIONS `champion_hold` (line 72) and `stand()` (lines 118-130): the holding champion stands one melee range (110) from the enemy lead minion, is "always a valid minion target", and has no health. With champion aggro on, every red minion that lacks a blue target hits it. The champion cannot die or leave, so "fragile" is not caused by the champion's death. Doc line 253 quotes 3,300 to 8,400 damage. Those are dt 1.0 medians from holds that mostly failed. The holds that pass at dt 0.5 and 0.25 (k=6) cost a median of 19,700 and 21,400. Real freezing positions to take aggro only when needed. Measure "health lost while freezing" (already on the C2 ground-truth list) against a champion that steps out of aggro range.

7. **Should-fix. The claim that the two design candidates agreed on the sampler is false.** Doc §12 (line 844): "Both chose... a reachable-position sampler for hidden states." `design_candidate_A.md` contains no hidden-state sampler at all: grep for "sampl", "hidden", and "reachab" finds nothing. Only candidate B has it (line 460). Decision log TSV line 13 lists the agreements without the sampler. The false agreement inflates confidence in a component that is otherwise Assumed (R9). Also, many of the agreements (route distance, no fight object, masks from observations) were dictated by the owner decisions and the roadmap that both candidates read. That makes "two independent designs that agree are stronger evidence than one" weaker than it sounds (see V7).

8. **Should-fix. Lateral slots are presented as a documented game rule.** Doc §5.6 line 235: "added only the two rules above that the real game has". In the same section, line 228 labels the lateral-slot targeting "(Assumed geometry)". Decision log TSV line 18 calls it "lane width so melee pair off", a documented rule. It was added after round 1 failed, and it is the rule that moved stability from 0/10 to 6/10 (at dt 1.0 only). This is the pattern the document warns against (handoff 001 F12). Label it as an assumption, and give it the same "must not exist only to produce a named outcome" test as any new rule.

9. **Should-fix. The provenance of every Sourced value lives outside the repository.** `patch_26_22.toml` line 9 points to "docs/design/M7_architecture.md, section Reference patch" for sources and conflicts. §3.1 lists only site-level sources and gives no citation per value. The per-value rows (for example `research_economy.md` B9c for the respawn conflict) are only in the session scratchpad, as are the decision log, both design candidates, and `run1.txt` and `run2.txt`. Handoff §5 admits the log is local. The planning assistant cannot do the F1 spot checks that R10 relies on, and a temp folder can disappear. Copy the two research tables, or a trimmed citation column, into `docs/design/` or the ruleset comments.

10. **Should-fix. §3.4 claims to list the reference rules M8 omits, but at least three omissions are missing.** (a) Wave composition shrink: one fewer melee on cannon waves from 14:00 and one fewer caster from 30:00 (`research_economy.md` A2c, fetched x2). The ruleset has no wave-composition key at all, and C7 runs 20-minute games. (b) Support-item poke charges: hitting an enemy champion or structure spends a charge for 18 gold (E20e, fetched x2). E20g rates poke as World Atlas's largest income channel (13 to 14 gold per 10 s against 10.5 for sharing), and the owner asked for a support income model. (c) Homeguard +150 percent after 14:00 or after a lane tower falls (`research_map.md` B7c). Add them to §3.4 or to the ruleset.

11. **Should-fix. The hidden-state sampler conflicts with public level.** Doc §9.3 (line 609): "A camp that a sampled enemy path passes may be marked cleared." §6.3 (line 432) makes each champion's level public. If the sampler credits the clear's gold and XP, the enemy jungler's level can change, and the deciding team's observation changes too. That fails the §9.3 acceptance test ("byte-identical"). If it does not credit them, the sampled world is inconsistent: a camp is cleared but nobody gained from it. The enemy team's memory and the enemy policy's view must also agree with the sampled path. The document needs a rule here before C8.

12. **Should-fix. Several M8 acceptance criteria cannot be checked as written, and two roadmap "Done when" clauses have no criterion.** Doc §10.1 (lines 658-665). (a) C2 "The M7 wave tests pass on the M8 code": those tests import `experiments.m7.waves.lane`, `scenarios`, and `CohortLane`, and one test pins prototype call signatures, while §13 says M8 "does not import it". (c) C2 bot-lane "less than 2.0 times, and each less than 1.0 times": the pair could earn 1.98 times a solo laner and still pass, so this barely tests "does not duplicate". (d) C2 "First waves meet within 5 percent of 21.8 s": the check is circular, because 21.8 comes from the same map data the simulator uses. Add the meeting time to the custom-game measurements. `research_economy.md` A1c's remembered figure (about 27 s at 325 speed) disagrees by about 15 percent. (e) C4 "a time that the handoff reports against the reference": no reference exists (R3), so there is nothing to pass or fail. (f) C5 "The M7 fight scenarios pass": these are bound to the prototype, and their thresholds are loose (0 to 8,000). (g) The C1 soak's invariants are not listed. (h) The roadmap M8 clauses "Wave, jungle, recall, and respawn timings interact coherently" and "Towers constrain waves and champions" map to no checkpoint criterion, yet "The M8 gate of the roadmap is met when C8 passes" (line 669).

13. **Should-fix. The pilot budget figures are optimistic and partly undefined.** Doc §10.3 (lines 725-738) and §10.2. "About 400 million agent decisions" per run uses the prototype's 634 simulated seconds per second. The C7 gate accepts 300, which the document itself says gives about 200 million (line 749). The prototype also used first-round lane rules and outer towers only, its minions never damaged champions, and its support income was passive gold only (handoff §3.5). This caveat is missing from §10.3. The arithmetic also assumes the learner overlaps the workers perfectly. A synchronous PPO loop like the predecessor's gives about 1/(1/12,000 + 1/30,000) ≈ 8,600 decisions per second, not 12,000. State the volume as a range of 150 to 400 million. The learner benchmark uses 64 actions against about 90 in §7.1 and 108 in the prototype. That is immaterial for cost, but it is inconsistent. The pilot reward's weights (plate and tower bonus, death penalty) and the metric for "beats the scripted baseline" (Q2) are not specified.

The 634 figure and the gate reference come only from `bench start` (`bench.py` lines 1-3 and 181). `kernel.json` is merged across separate runs (`scenarios.py` line 349, `bench.py` line 180), and no results file records a timestamp, code state, or command. `q5_throughput` predates the second-round lane rules.

### Judgment calls

15. **Judgment call. The fight evidence is thinner than "Verified".** Handoff F7 (line 92), doc §5.9 (lines 325-332). In the retreat sweep the scripted chaser never disengages: 11 of the 15 "escapes" end with the chaser dead (`chaser_dead: true`), mostly to the runner's tower. At 1,000 units, speed edge and lockdown change nothing: the runner's lowest health is 238 in all six cases. Lockdown flips the outcome in one of nine pairs. In the reinforcement sweep the losing champion dies in every case, even with the ally starting at 0 units (`b_largest_ally_distance_a_survives: null`). The sweep is monotone (I reran it), so "within 2,000, loses from 2,250" is correct. The phrase "retreat is neither guaranteed nor hopeless" mostly reflects a suicidal chaser.

16. **Judgment call. The map's "independent" check is a consistency check.** Doc §5.1 (lines 138-147), §3.5 (line 112), handoff F3. The reference figures (`research_map.md` lines 214-234) come from the same Community Dragon minion polylines as the route graph's lane nodes. The check therefore validates the graph's down-sampling, not travel times in the real game. `tests/test_map.py` line 25 uses `rel=0.04`, so the test enforces 4 percent, not the 3 percent claimed.

17. **Judgment call. The cohort rejection rests on a mode artifact.** Doc §5.6 line 233: the cohort model gives "0.67 where the rules imply 0.50". I reproduced 0.667 and 0.50. But 0.50 follows from the prototype's `last_hit` mode, which hits only minions that one hit will kill and so never prepares a caster for the tower shot. Real players do prepare casters. No results file holds the cohort numbers. A rerun on a loaded machine gave about 28 against 81 microseconds, not 25 against 67. The decision (individual minions) is still reasonable on the "cannot express per-minion rules" ground.

18. **Judgment call. The pilot comes late.** Doc §10.2 (line 673). The pilot comes after C7, which needs C1 to C6, so it waits for every subsystem checkpoint. That honours the owner's "not automatically after every M8 deliverable" only in the literal sense, since only C8 remains. The rationale is sound (C7 is the first integrated game), but no earlier option is weighed, such as a pilot after C2, C3, and C5 on lanes, recall, and combat, with fog stubbed.

19. **Judgment call. M8 scope is expanded to three tower tiers.** Doc §5.7 (line 293) puts outer, inner, and inhibitor towers in M8 (18 towers). The roadmap says "Basic lane towers", and the owner put "fuller structure progression" in M10. This is disclosed as handoff R2, but it adds work to C2 and C5 and needs the "removed at base" stand-in (line 300).

20. **Judgment call. The LANE restriction (D4) removes decisions the evaluator targets.** Doc §5.10 (lines 343-350) forbids LANE actions outside the home lane until an outer tower falls or 14:00. Lane swaps and early side-lane choices then cannot be expressed in the pilot window (episodes are the first 14 minutes). The faithful option is the reference's off-lane penalties. The choice is disclosed and defensible for M8, but it shapes the pilot's action space.

21. **Judgment call. M7 wrote about 3,700 lines of code.** That compares with the roadmap's M7 "Not in scope: Large-scale coding". The owner's prompt authorises prototypes, and handoff §4.1 discloses this. Note it for the roadmap's M7 wording.

**G. The wave result.** The conclusion is overstated for freezes and unsupported by its own scenario for slow pushes. At the M8 tick of 0.5 s, and at the 0.25 s reference, the prototype's pre-registered `emerged()` test reports `freeze: true` (item 1). The negative exists only at the 1.0 s reporting tick, which the document never names in its wave tables. The holds that pass are not realistic freezes either. They rely on an immortal champion who stands at melee range and absorbs about 20,000 damage (item 6). So "fragile and too costly" describes the standing rule as much as the wave rules. The slow-push scenario S4b has an obvious flaw. It starts about 3,000 units from the enemy tower, thins one wave only, and stops at the first minion to arrive, so the remnant reaches the tower before any reinforcement can (item 4). The better evidence that waves do not stack is S1b, which the document classes as instability. Deferring to C2 with ground-truth measurements is the right process. Two documented rules that M7 did not test bear directly on the identified 7 s reinforcement gap in top lane: the side-lane speed boost and call-for-help. Restate the conflict as: "freeze-like holds appear at fine ticks under an assumed standing rule, and stacking was not shown in a fair scenario."

The manifest has two dependency quirks (item 33).
